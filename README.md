
Capstone Project title: Analysisof Heatwave and mortality in Europe  

This capstone project examines the relationship between heatwaves and mortality in Europe between 2000 and 2023. It combines two datasets, i.e. i) a gridded climate dataset of monthly high UTCI- Universal Thermal Climate Index- days as an indicator of heat stress, and ii) Eurostat weekly mortality data for European countries.  High UTCI days represent the number of days experiencing significant thermal stress, ranging from moderate (26°C–32°C) to extreme (>46°C), based on the Universal Thermal Climate Index. This index calculates human heat exchange using air temperature, humidity, wind, and radiation. The project first analyzes how heatwave conditions evolved across Europe over time, including annual, summer, and spatial patterns. It then estimates excess mortality by comparing observed weekly deaths with a historical baseline and focuses on summer weeks to identify mortality peaks during major heatwave years. By bringing the two datasets together, this project aims to show whether years with stronger summer heat stress were also associated with higher mortality burdens across Europe.

Environmental Research Questions:

How did heatwave evolve across Europe during 2000-2023?
Who is most vulnerable, and what were the measurable health impacts (excess mortality)?


Main findings

The climate analysis shows that heatwave-related heat stress increased across Europe during 2000–2023, with clear year-to-year variability and stronger summer exposure in well-known extreme years such as 2003, 2010, 2015, 2018, and 2022. Spatial patterns indicate that heatwave conditions were not uniform across the continent, with stronger burdens in parts of southern, western, and central Europe.

The mortality analysis shows that summer excess deaths rose markedly in major heatwave years, although the magnitude differed across countries. When measured in raw summer excess deaths, the countries with the largest burdens were mostly Germany and Spain, with Poland, the Netherlands, Portugal, and Switzerland also appearing repeatedly among the most affected countries in several heatwave years. In particular, Germany dominated raw excess deaths in 2010, 2015, 2018, and 2022, while Spain was especially prominent in 2003 and 2022.

When mortality is standardized as percent excess mortality, smaller countries appear more prominently. Countries such as Iceland, Liechtenstein, Andorra, Luxembourg, and Switzerland showed very high percentage increases in some years, while Spain, Portugal, Germany, and the Netherlands also recorded substantial relative impacts in major heatwave summers. This indicates that the interpretation of results depends on whether impact is measured in absolute deaths or relative mortality increase.

Overall, the findings suggest a clear association between severe summer heatwave conditions and elevated mortality in Europe. The project also highlights an important methodological point: large countries dominate absolute mortality counts, while smaller countries may show stronger relative mortality responses. Together, the results support the conclusion that heatwaves are a major and unevenly distributed public-health risk across Europe.

Short limitations note

A key limitation is that the mortality data represent all-cause deaths, not medically certified heat deaths, so the analysis estimates excess mortality during hot periods rather than direct heat-attributable mortality. In addition, the UTCI data are monthly, which captures seasonal heat burden well but is less precise for very short heatwave events.
