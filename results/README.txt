Main findings

The climate analysis shows that heatwave-related heat stress increased across Europe during 2000–2023, with clear year-to-year variability and stronger summer exposure in well-known extreme years such as 2003, 2010, 2015, 2018, and 2022. Spatial patterns indicate that heatwave conditions were not uniform across the continent, with stronger burdens in parts of southern, western, and central Europe.

The mortality analysis shows that summer excess deaths rose markedly in major heatwave years, although the magnitude differed across countries. When measured in raw summer excess deaths, the countries with the largest burdens were mostly Germany and Spain, with Poland, the Netherlands, Portugal, and Switzerland also appearing repeatedly among the most affected countries in several heatwave years. In particular, Germany dominated raw excess deaths in 2010, 2015, 2018, and 2022, while Spain was especially prominent in 2003 and 2022.

When mortality is standardized as percent excess mortality, smaller countries appear more prominently. Countries such as Iceland, Liechtenstein, Andorra, Luxembourg, and Switzerland showed very high percentage increases in some years, while Spain, Portugal, Germany, and the Netherlands also recorded substantial relative impacts in major heatwave summers. This indicates that the interpretation of results depends on whether impact is measured in absolute deaths or relative mortality increase.
